Online classes, office work and evening phone use mean many families now spend hours a day looking at screens. Eyes that feel dry, gritty or heavy by evening, blurred vision after work, and headaches across the forehead are common complaints. Doctors often call this digital eye strain.

## Why screens tire the eyes

When we concentrate on a screen we blink far less often than usual, so the surface of the eye dries out. Holding focus at one short distance for a long time also tires the focusing muscles. Glare, a screen that is too close and poor posture add to the strain.

Strain is usually uncomfortable rather than harmful, but the same symptoms can sometimes point to an uncorrected vision problem that glasses would help.

## Habits that may help

- **Look away regularly.** A common rule is 20-20-20: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
- **Blink on purpose** during long sessions, especially while reading or gaming.
- **Set the distance.** A computer screen at roughly an arm's length, slightly below eye level; hold phones a little further away than feels natural.
- **Reduce glare.** Avoid a bright window directly behind or in front of the screen, and match screen brightness to the room.
- **Take breaks outdoors.** Time outside in daylight is good for children's eyes as well as their health generally.
- **Mind the bedtime screen.** Screens late at night can disturb sleep, and tired eyes feel worse the next day.

## Children and screens

Children may not say their eyes are tired. Look for rubbing, squinting, holding a book or phone very close, headaches after school, or losing their place while reading. These are reasons to have their eyes checked.

## When to book an eye check

- blurred or double vision that does not settle with rest
- frequent headaches linked to reading or screen work
- red, painful or very watery eyes
- difficulty seeing the board at school, or road signs while driving
- you have diabetes or high blood pressure and have not had a recent eye check

**Sudden loss of vision, flashes with a shower of floaters, or an eye injury need urgent hospital care.**

At the clinic, the doctor checks your vision and eye health and advises whether glasses, eye drops or simple changes may help, or whether a referral is needed.
