Many people put off asking about implants because they expect to be pushed into a long, expensive treatment on the first day. A consultation is nothing of the sort. It is a conversation and an examination that ends with information, not a commitment.

## Before you come

It helps to bring:

- a list of the medicines you take, including blood thinners, diabetes tablets and any treatment for bone thinning (osteoporosis)
- recent blood reports if you have them
- any old dental X-rays or scans
- a note of what bothers you most: chewing, appearance, a loose denture, or all three

## What the dentist asks

Expect questions about your general health, because it affects healing. Diabetes, smoking, some medicines and past radiotherapy to the head or neck all matter. None of these automatically rules implants out, but they change the plan and sometimes the timing.

You will also be asked how the tooth was lost, how long ago, and what you have tried since.

## What the dentist checks

- **Gums:** active gum disease needs treating first, because it can affect implants too.
- **Bone:** an X-ray, or sometimes a 3D scan, measures the jawbone's height and width and maps nearby nerves and sinuses.
- **Bite and neighbouring teeth:** the new tooth must fit the way you close and chew.
- **Space:** there has to be enough room for the implant and the tooth on top of it.

## The options you may hear about

An implant is one way to fill a gap. Depending on your situation, the dentist may also discuss a fixed bridge, a removable partial denture, or leaving the gap alone. A good consultation covers the pros and cons of each, including the number of visits and the likely cost.

## Questions worth asking

- Am I a suitable candidate now, or does something need to be treated first?
- How many visits, and over how many months?
- Will I have a temporary tooth while it heals?
- What does the cost include, and what could change it?
- How should I look after the implant once it is finished?

## After the visit

You leave with an explanation and, if you wish, a written plan. Take time to think it over and discuss it with your family. Treatment begins only when you are ready.

*General information only. Suitability for implants can only be decided by a dentist after examining you.*
