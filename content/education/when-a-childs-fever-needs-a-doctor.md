Fever is one of the most common reasons parents bring a child to a doctor. In many cases it is the body's normal response to an infection such as a cold. The number on the thermometer matters less than how your child looks and behaves. This guide is general information only; if you are worried, it is always reasonable to have your child seen.

## Measuring the temperature

A digital thermometer under the arm is simple and safe for most children. A temperature of about 38°C or more is usually counted as a fever. Write down the readings and the times; the doctor will find them useful.

## Keeping your child comfortable at home

- Dress them in light clothes and avoid heavy blankets.
- Offer drinks often: water, breast milk or formula for babies, and fluids they will accept.
- Let them rest; they do not need to eat much for a day or two if they are drinking.
- Use fever medicine only as a doctor has advised for your child's age and weight, and never give two medicines at once without advice.
- Sponging with cold water is not recommended; it can make a child shiver and feel worse.

## See a doctor the same day if

- your baby is **under three months old** and has a fever
- the fever has lasted **more than two or three days**
- your child is **not drinking** or is passing much less urine than usual
- they have **ear pain**, a **painful throat** with difficulty swallowing, or **pain passing urine**
- the fever keeps coming back, or you simply feel something is not right

## Go to a hospital emergency department straight away if

- your child is **very drowsy, floppy or hard to wake**
- they are **breathing fast**, grunting or the skin between the ribs pulls in
- they have a **fit** (convulsion)
- a **rash does not fade** when you press a glass against it
- their lips look blue or grey, or their hands and feet are cold and mottled

## At the clinic

The doctor will ask about the fever and other symptoms, examine your child and explain the likely cause. Many fevers settle with care at home; some need tests or antibiotics, and a few need hospital care. You will leave knowing which signs to watch for and when to come back.

*General information only, not a substitute for seeing a doctor. In an emergency, go to the nearest hospital.*
