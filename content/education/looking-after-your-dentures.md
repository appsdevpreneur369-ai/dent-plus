A well-made denture can last for years, but only if it is cleaned and handled with a little care. This guide covers the daily routine our prosthodontists suggest to most patients. Your own dentist may adjust it for your mouth.

## The first few weeks

New dentures feel bulky at first. Extra saliva, a slight lisp and sore spots on the gums are common while the mouth adapts. Start with soft food cut into small pieces, chew on both sides at once, and read aloud for a few minutes a day to get your speech back to normal.

A sore spot that does not settle within a few days is a reason to come back, not to put up with it. Easing a pressure point takes the dentist only a few minutes.

## A simple daily routine

- **After meals:** take the denture out and rinse away food over a basin half-filled with water, so it does not crack if you drop it.
- **Once a day:** brush every surface of the denture with a soft brush and a denture cleaner or mild liquid soap. Ordinary toothpaste is often too gritty and leaves fine scratches where stains collect.
- **Your own mouth:** brush any natural teeth you still have, and gently clean your gums and tongue with a soft brush.
- **At night:** most people are advised to leave dentures out while sleeping to rest the gums. Store them in a covered container of clean water so they do not dry out and change shape.

## Things to avoid

- Hot or boiling water, which can warp the plastic.
- Bleach or strong household cleaners, which can weaken and discolour it.
- Home repairs with shop-bought glue. Bring a cracked or broken denture to the clinic instead.
- Wrapping a denture in tissue at a meal. It is the most common way dentures end up in the dustbin.

## When to see the dentist

Gums slowly change shape over the years, so a denture that once fitted well can start to rock or slip. Come in if you notice:

- looseness, clicking or food getting trapped underneath
- sores, red patches or cracks at the corners of your mouth
- a chip, crack or a tooth coming away from the base
- difficulty chewing that is getting worse

A loose denture can often be relined to fit again. For people who struggle with a lower denture that will not stay in place, a small number of implants may be an option; the dentist can tell you whether that suits you.

*General information only. Your dentist will advise on care for your own denture.*
